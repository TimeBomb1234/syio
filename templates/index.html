Light: Reflection and Refraction

  • CBSE
  • Class 10
  • Science

Chapter summary

This chapter explains how light behaves when it bounces off surfaces and when it passes from one medium into another. You learn how mirrors and lenses form images, and how to predict their position and size using sign conventions and formulas.

Key concepts

  • Light travels in straight lines and reflects according to the laws of reflection.
  • Spherical mirrors are concave or convex, and each forms different kinds of images.
  • Refraction is the bending of light as it changes speed between media.
  • Lenses use refraction to focus or spread light, which sets their power.

Keywords and formulas

  • Focal length
  • Principal axis
  • Snell's law
  • 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
  • m = h′/h = −v/u
  • Power = 1/f

Practice questions

State the two laws of reflection.
The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane.
What kind of mirror is used as a car's rear-view mirror, and why?
A convex mirror, because it always forms an upright, diminished image and gives a wider field of view.
An object is 20 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Where does the image form?
Using the mirror formula with u = −20 cm and f = −15 cm, v = −60 cm. The image forms 60 cm in front of the mirror.
